1. Our Purpose and Scope
EDITED_POLICY_SENTINEL: This complete edited paragraph must be exported.
This policy applies to AI use for business purposes, including use on personal accounts, personal
devices, external supplier platforms and AI features embedded in other software. A tool being free,
familiar or widely available does not make it approved.
It applies to all employees and to contractors, temporary workers and other representatives who use AI
for the business. Managers must ensure that people working under their supervision receive the policy
and understand the requirements relevant to their role. Existing confidentiality, security, employment
and professional obligations continue to apply.
“AI tools” means services and features that generate, transform, analyse or recommend content or
actions using artificial intelligence. “Approved” means explicitly authorised by the business for a stated
purpose and account arrangement, not merely technically accessible. “High-impact” work includes work
that could materially affect a person’s opportunities, rights, finances, health, safety or treatment, or the
business’s legal or operational position.

3. Acceptable Use of AI
The reported areas of AI use are: Writing and content creation; Human resources and recruitment;
Customer support. AI may support work only within these authorised purposes and the employee’s
normal responsibilities. Any materially different use must be considered through the tool-authorisation
process. AI output is assistance, not a substitute for professional judgement or accountability.
For customer support, employees must verify AI-suggested responses against current, reliable business
information before sending them. Customer personal information must not be entered into unapproved
tools. Customers must be given a route to a human for complex queries, complaints, distress, disputed
outcomes or requests outside the tool’s scope; the business must confirm that route. AI must not make
unauthorised commitments, guarantees, refunds or eligibility decisions. [CUSTOMER ESCALATION
ROUTE TO BE CONFIRMED]
4. Prohibited Use of AI
Employees must not use AI to deceive, harass, discriminate, exploit, impersonate or unlawfully monitor
people; generate unlawful material; infringe privacy or intellectual-property rights; or bypass legal,
contractual, security or professional obligations. AI must not be used to create misleading evidence,
false records, fabricated sources or unauthorised representations of the business.
Do not use unapproved tools for business information, share credentials with a model, circumvent
access controls, install unauthorised AI extensions or grant access to company information without the
authorisation required by this policy. Do not submit restricted information in an attempt to test
whether a tool will protect it.
Employees must not represent AI-generated content as independently verified fact, use AI to make the
high-impact decisions reserved for humans in this policy, or deploy autonomous actions outside the
approved purpose. Where a requested use is unclear or conflicts with this policy, pause and seek
guidance before proceeding.

5. Data Protection and Confidential Information
Employees must not enter the following into unapproved AI tools, including prompts, attachments,
training settings or connected applications: Customer personal information; Passwords and login
credentials. When an AI tool’s approval status is unclear, treat it as unapproved and ask management
before sharing information.
Use the minimum information necessary. Remove or replace identifying and confidential details where
the task allows, and prefer anonymised or synthetic examples. Removing a name alone does not
necessarily make information anonymous. Approval to use an AI tool is not blanket permission to
disclose every category of business information.
Passwords, login credentials, secret keys, authentication codes and recovery codes must never be
pasted into an AI prompt or exposed through an AI integration. Personal, customer, regulated and
confidential information remains subject to applicable confidentiality, privacy and contractual
obligations even when an AI tool is approved.
Before uploading company documents to any AI tool, employees must obtain prior management
approval for the proposed upload or a clearly defined class of uploads. The approval must identify the
documents, tool and account plan, purpose and any handling restrictions, and must be recorded.
Approval of a tool alone is not approval to upload documents. [DOCUMENT UPLOAD APPROVAL
RECORD LOCATION TO BE CONFIRMED]
Permitted uploads remain subject to the restrictions above. Before uploading, employees must check
relevant supplier terms, retention, deletion and model-training settings where available, and must not
assume that an approved supplier provides protections that have not been verified.
This policy applies to operations in Nigeria and the technology context described by the business. The
business must identify the local laws, contracts and sector-specific obligations relevant to its actual
activities. This draft does not certify compliance with any particular legal regime.
Connections to company systems, applications or databases require a documented security review
before activation and explicit authorisation by management. The review must be performed by a
person with appropriate security competence; if that expertise is not available internally, the business
must obtain it before proceeding. [SECURITY REVIEW OWNER AND EVIDENCE LOCATION TO BE
CONFIRMED]
Connections must use the least access necessary, with read-only access preferred where sufficient. The
reviewer or approver must consider authentication, supplier handling of information, data flows,
logging, deletion, error handling and a way to revoke access. Secrets must not be embedded in prompts
or shared directly with an AI model. Access must be removed when no longer needed.
Any material expansion of permissions, new write access, new data sources or autonomous actions
requires renewed approval and review. Employees must report unexpected actions or access promptly.
Approval of a connection does not permit employees to bypass other policy restrictions.

6. Human Oversight and Accuracy
All AI-generated work must be reviewed by a human before use, whether it is used internally or shared
externally. This includes text, code, analysis, images, summaries, recommendations and automated
actions. The reviewing employee is responsible for checking factual accuracy, completeness, relevance,
bias, confidentiality, rights and fitness for the intended purpose. A person must understand the output
sufficiently to take responsibility for its use and must correct or reject unsupported content.
The following activities always require human oversight: Recruitment and hiring decisions; Public
communications. A suitably informed person must review the relevant AI contribution and remain
accountable for the final decision or action. AI must not be the sole decision-maker for these activities.
For recruitment and hiring, a trained human must make or substantively validate screening, shortlisting,
interview, selection and rejection decisions. AI scores and recommendations are advisory only.
Reviewers must check evidence, use job-related criteria, consider relevant bias and accessibility risks,
and provide an appropriate route to request human reconsideration. Do not infer protected
characteristics or upload candidate information to unapproved tools. Record the human decision and its
rationale in accordance with applicable requirements.
Public communications and important business reports must be checked by an authorised reviewer for
factual support, appropriate tone, confidentiality and material omissions before release. Significant
claims, figures, quotations and recommendations must be traced to reliable sources.
7. AI-Generated Content and Disclosure
Employees must disclose AI-generated or substantially AI-assisted content in external communications,
including customer, client, supplier and public-facing content. Disclosure should be clear and
appropriate to the medium. This rule does not remove any additional disclosure obligation imposed by
applicable rules or contracts.
Employees must check permissions, attribution and licensing before using generated content and must
not assume AI output is free of third-party rights or automatically protected by copyright. Do not
present fabricated quotations, sources, testimonials or evidence as genuine. Remove or correct
inaccurate content promptly and retain source material where required by normal business
record-keeping rules.
8. Account Security and Access Control
The business reported use of personal accounts. A personal account is not automatically approved for
business use. The business must confirm which personal accounts, tools and plans may be used and for
what purposes. Unless a particular personal account and use have been explicitly approved with
appropriate protections, treat it as unapproved and do not enter restricted business information,
upload company documents or connect it to company systems.
For any explicitly approved personal-account use, employees must maintain a strong, unique password,
enable multi-factor authentication where available, keep recovery details private and prevent

9. Incident Reporting
No formal AI-related incident reporting process was reported. Recommendation: before adopting this
policy, establish a clear reporting route, a responsible owner and a fallback for urgent incidents. Until
that route is formally confirmed, this draft proposes immediate reporting to management as an interim
measure; it does not claim that a formal process already exists. [REPORTING OWNER, CONTACT
METHOD AND FALLBACK TO BE CONFIRMED]
Report accidental disclosure of sensitive information, suspected account compromise, suspicious
AI-related activity, unsafe integrations and material errors as soon as they are noticed. Do not wait for
complete evidence or delay because you are unsure whether an incident qualifies. If the normal route is
unavailable, use the confirmed fallback or escalate to management.
Stop the affected activity where it is safe to do so, prevent further disclosure and preserve relevant
evidence such as time, tool, prompts, outputs and screenshots without circulating sensitive material
more widely. Do not delete evidence, investigate beyond your authority or attempt to conceal the
incident. Cooperate with the designated response owner.
The responsible business owner must assess the incident, arrange containment and recovery, and
determine whether any customer, regulator, insurer, supplier or other notification is required under
applicable obligations. Employees must not make external notifications or public statements about an
incident unless authorised, except where applicable law gives them an independent right or duty to
report.
10. Employee Responsibilities
Employees are responsible for understanding this policy, using only authorised tools and access
arrangements, protecting information, checking outputs, meeting disclosure requirements and
reporting concerns. The person using or approving AI-assisted work remains responsible for its quality
and consequences; responsibility cannot be assigned to the AI tool.

Employees must follow the restrictions on document uploads, system connections and human review
set out in this policy. Ask management when approval status or a rule is unclear. Do not treat a lack of

Managers must communicate relevant restrictions, support employees in raising concerns and ensure

11. AI Training and Awareness
Employees must complete responsible AI training before using AI tools for business purposes and
participate in periodic refresher training thereafter. Initial training must cover the approved
environment and essential safeguards; refreshers must address changes in tools, risks, incidents and
responsibilities. [TRAINING OWNER, REFRESHER FREQUENCY AND COMPLETION RECORD TO BE
CONFIRMED]
Training and awareness activities should use practical, role-relevant examples and explain how to pause
work and seek help. Employees must not treat completion of training as permission to ignore
tool-specific restrictions or professional review requirements.
12. Policy Review and Updates
This policy must be reviewed every 6 months. The business must also review it when significant
changes in tools, risks, incidents, operations or applicable obligations make an earlier review necessary.
The review should consider whether approved tools and account plans remain appropriate, whether
controls and restrictions are workable, what incidents or employee feedback have revealed, and
whether oversight, training and reporting arrangements need updating. The business should check
relevant obligations in its country and industry rather than assuming this draft is legally complete.
Material updates must be approved through the business’s appropriate internal process, dated and
communicated to affected employees. The business should maintain a version history and confirm
whether renewed acknowledgement or training is needed. [POLICY REVIEW OWNER AND VERSION
